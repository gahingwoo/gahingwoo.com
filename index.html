    About

    Linux runs most of the world's servers and every Android phone. Every change to it is reviewed in public by a maintainer who can refuse it. Five of mine are in mainline, merged in the 7.3 window and present since v7.3-rc1, and the review threads are linked below.

    Year 13 student at Mount Albert Grammar School in Auckland, working on firmware and embedded Linux, mostly on Rockchip silicon that nobody had brought up yet. Beyond the kernel, the first OP-TEE port for the RK3576 and a Trusted Firmware-A secure-boot fix are merged too.

    Every claim on this site links to the public record behind it. Where an earlier claim of mine turned out to be wrong, the record says so.

    In mainline Linux

    You cannot put code into Linux yourself. It goes in only if the maintainer who owns that part of the kernel takes it, and that happens on a public mailing list where a refusal is as visible as an acceptance and the archive keeps every revision, including the ones that were wrong. Nothing in that thread counts except the patch. What they took is below, each line naming the maintainer who applied it: memory protection for the chip's AI accelerator, support for two new boards, and a fix to Ethernet timing.

    iommu/rockchip
    Two fixes for the RK3576 NPU's IOMMU instancesApplied by Will Deacon, IOMMU co-maintainer. Mainline, v7.3-rc1.
    arm64 dts
    ArmSoM CM5 and CM5-IO board supportApplied by Heiko Stübner, Rockchip SoC maintainer; Acked-by Krzysztof Kozlowski. Mainline, v7.3-rc1.
    net/phy motorcomm
    Reference clock for the YT8521 PHYApplied by Jakub Kicinski, net maintainer; Reviewed-by Andrew Lunn. Mainline, v7.3-rc1.

    Firmware and trusted execution

    Firmware is the code that runs before the operating system starts. A trusted execution environment is a sealed-off part of the chip that keeps holding keys and fingerprints even if the operating system itself is broken into. This chip had no such environment until the port below, which the project that maintains the standard reviewed and merged. The rest is secure boot, the same kind of isolation on a second chip, and firmware that boots an ordinary Fedora desktop.

    OP-TEE
    First RK3576 platform portMerged. Reviewed-by Cherry Embedded Solutions; run by Flipper Devices on Flipper One, 35,681 subtests, zero failures.
    Trusted Firmware-A
    Redundant secure-boot override removedMerged. Reviewed by engineers at STMicroelectronics, Arm and Rockchip.
    RP2350
    TrustZone-M isolation exampleSecureFault verified over SWD rather than trusted from source.
    UEFI
    edk2-rk3576Boots a mainline Fedora 44 desktop; pinned on the Radxa forum by a Radxa engineer.

    What is next

    Nothing here is done. Each line says where the work stands today and what finishing it would mean, so the distance between the two is stated rather than left to the reader.

    RK3576 NPU
    Into the accel treeThe 14-patch series is at v12 with the driver's author reviewing. The goal is for it to be applied, so the RK3576 NPU works on a stock kernel.
    Mesa
    Depthwise, pointwise and image-input convolutions!43804 covers one regular convolution on purpose. The layer types MobileNet is actually built from follow in later series.
    charsiu
    An open runtime that matches or beats the vendor'sNine models already run end to end on the mainline driver with output identical to the CPU reference. The goal is performance at or above the closed vendor stack, with no vendor code in the path.
    Video encoder
    Rebase the VEPU510 driver on the DRM/Vulkan-Video splitThe stateful V4L2 interface was abandoned on the maintainers' advice; the driver returns once the RK3588 split it will sit on has landed.
    UEFI
    A complete UEFI for the RK3576edk2-rk3576 boots a mainline Fedora 44 desktop today. The goal is a full, polished firmware: every board it builds for verified, and the platform support upstreamable.
