RK3576 NPU on mainline Linux

Brought up the RK3576's neural accelerator on the open rocket driver, working from the vendor's model files rather than any documentation. The bug that blocked chained compute for 38 days was one register field: a 16-bit task count where the RK3588 header assumed 12. The 14-patch series is at v11 with the driver's author reviewing it, and two IOMMU fixes split out of it are in Linux 7.3. The preprint's second version retracts the first version's central claim and explains how the method held a false result.

series, v11 preprint, DOI write-up

Board bring-up that reached Linux 7.3

Devicetree support for the ArmSoM CM5 and CM5-IO, applied by Heiko Stübner after five review rounds, with an Acked-by on the binding from Krzysztof Kozlowski. A reference-clock fix for the Motorcomm YT8521 PHY, applied by Jakub Kicinski with a Reviewed-by from Andrew Lunn and a Tested-by from the owner of a different RK3576 board. My first attempt at that fix lived in the MAC driver. Two maintainers said the PHY should own the clock, so I withdrew it and rewrote it at the right layer.

Trusted execution, Cortex-A down to Cortex-M

The first OP-TEE port for the RK3576, merged with a Reviewed-by from a Cherry Embedded Solutions maintainer and later run by Flipper Devices on their own hardware: 114 xtest cases, 35,681 subtests, zero failures. A Trusted Firmware-A fix for a copy-paste defect that would have silently broken secure boot, reviewed by engineers at STMicroelectronics, Arm and Rockchip. On the RP2350, a TrustZone-M isolation example whose fault handling was checked over SWD rather than trusted from source.

Tools other people ship

SoC-Consistency checks a devicetree's power tree for misconfiguration before it reaches hardware. It is on PyPI and was packaged into Arch Linux by a maintainer I have never met. RKDevelopTool-GUI, a flashing front end for Rockchip boards, has its own page in Radxa's documentation. kiln, a stopgap for running local language models on the RK3576 NPU, has a full Radxa tutorial. edk2-rk3576, a UEFI port that boots Fedora 44, was pinned on the Radxa forum by one of their engineers.
